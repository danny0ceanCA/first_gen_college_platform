# Origen recommended agreements: owner and reviewer guide

Prepared October 4, 2026. This packet is ready for your review and for sharing with a legal friend or legal-aid reviewer. It does not require purchasing legal services to continue drafting. It is a proposed agreement and notice, not a certification that every requirement has been met.

## Read these documents

1. [Terms of Use](TERMS_OF_USE.md): the agreement about using Origen.
2. [Privacy Notice](PRIVACY_NOTICE.md): what information is processed, why, who receives it, and users' controls.
3. [Registration and publication plan](REGISTRATION_AND_PUBLICATION.md): where notices belong and how to record acceptance.

## Recommended choices already included

- Public Terms and Privacy links on the landing page, with access from registration and Settings.
- One unchecked registration box accepting the Terms and acknowledging receipt of the Privacy Notice. Acknowledgment does not waive rights or authorize every possible data use.
- A short disclosure before voice starts, including preview voice. No repeated legal forms during the conversation.
- Clear identification of AI guidance and summaries, their potential errors, and the need to verify important school and financial-aid information.
- Users retain rights in their content; Origen receives permission needed to operate the requested features.
- No compulsory arbitration or class-action waiver. A proposed liability cap is the greater of $1,000 or twelve months of fees, with exceptions for unlawful personal-data handling, confidentiality, serious misconduct, and mandatory rights. A narrow third-party-claim reimbursement provision applies to institutions, not consumer families.
- Proposed 13+ independent account/voice use, with guardian involvement for minors as required. This choice still needs implementation and review; parent-created profiles about younger children also need assessment.
- Honest deletion and retention limitations instead of promises that all providers immediately erase every copy.

## Details you can supply yourself

- The actual legal operator. If you have no company, identify the individual operating the service; do not name a company that does not exist.
- A suitable mailing address and a monitored support/privacy email. The application currently defaults to `daniel@origenedu.ai`; confirm whether that should handle privacy requests.
- Intended markets and whether users will be limited to the United States initially.
- The date the finalized documents will take effect.
- Whether you plan to serve children under 13, accept school-provided records, charge users, or introduce advertising. Those choices change the review needed.

Do not fill uncertain provider practices with guesses. Verify the actual OpenAI account settings and agreements, Auth0 identity-deletion process, and Render backup/log retention. The Privacy Notice currently flags those gaps so a reviewer can see them. Remove internal review notes from public copy only after replacing them with accurate disclosures.

## Focused questions for a legal friend or legal-aid reviewer

You can send the following with the packet:

> Origen is a college-guidance application for families and students. It uses Auth0 login, Render hosting/PostgreSQL, and OpenAI text and live voice. It saves profiles, plans, and AI-generated conversation summaries. Preview voice can be used before registration. I have prepared the attached recommended Terms and Privacy Notice and would appreciate a focused review of:
>
> 1. The operator identification, formation of the agreement, and proposed governing-law language.
> 2. Minor accounts, guardian permission, and parent-created profiles, including information about children under 13.
> 3. Voice disclosures and whether additional consent is required for the intended users and processing.
> 4. Applicable state privacy obligations, notice at collection, and request/appeal procedures.
> 5. The distinction between direct consumer guidance and school-authorized use of protected student records.
> 6. Whether the content license, service limitations, account closure, and liability provisions are fair and enforceable.
>
> The documents flag unverified provider retention and security practices rather than claiming compliance. Please identify the highest-priority corrections and any operational changes needed before publication.
