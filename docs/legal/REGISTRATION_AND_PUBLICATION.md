# Origen legal notices and registration plan

Prepared October 4, 2026. These are reviewable drafts, not published policies or implemented consent controls.

## Recommended placement

Use **both public links and registration acceptance**. Put separate Terms of Use and Privacy Notice links in the landing-page footer, registration screen, and signed-in Settings. Give each a stable, directly accessible URL, readable on mobile without login. Replace the existing short privacy overview with a plain-language summary linking to the full Notice, or retain the overview above those links.

At first account registration, use an unchecked checkbox next to:

> I agree to the [Terms of Use] and acknowledge the [Privacy Notice].

Spanish copy for the control, once matching reviewed Spanish documents are available:

> Acepto los [Términos de uso] y reconozco haber recibido el [Aviso de privacidad].

Use a clear **Create account** or **Agree and create account** button. Do not use an unexplained “OK,” a prechecked checkbox, or a notice hidden below the button. Make the links readable and keyboard accessible. Do not require users to scroll through the entire document as proof of reading. Privacy acknowledgment records delivery of a notice; it is not blanket consent to all data processing.

This is a one-time registration step, not a confirmation form during conversations. Returning users should only be asked again when a material Terms update requires renewed assent. Do not conflate accepting Terms, hearing the welcome introduction, or granting microphone permission.

## Voice and preview notice

Show a concise notice beside the Start voice button before microphone capture, including signed-out preview:

> Origen uses an AI-generated voice. Starting sends your microphone audio and relevant conversation context to OpenAI to provide guidance and summaries. Avoid sensitive details. You can stop anytime. [Privacy Notice]

Button: **Start voice conversation**. The button can express the disclosed action without adding repetitive confirmation dialogs. Have counsel assess whether separate express consent is required for the jurisdictions, recording practices, or sensitive data involved. Browser microphone permission alone does not settle those requirements.

Show a preview reminder: “Preview information may stay in this browser. Avoid private information on shared devices.” Do not delay notice until registration, because preview voice can collect information first. Where privacy law requires notice at collection, present the relevant categories, purposes, retention criteria, and rights link at or before collection.

## Acceptance receipt implementation

Before allowing first account setup, record acceptance on the authenticated server, in PostgreSQL. Suggested fields: account ID, Terms version, Privacy Notice version, acceptance timestamp generated by the server, interface locale, and acceptance source. Preserve immutable published document versions. Validate the submitted version and explicit affirmative action server-side; do not rely on browser storage. Do not store extra IP/device fingerprints solely to create a receipt without a justified purpose and disclosed retention.

Keep legal acceptance separate from `welcome_heard_at` and `voice_used_at`. Those fields describe experience, not contractual agreement or privacy consent. Handle existing accounts with a clear first-use acceptance screen when the finalized Terms launch. Request new assent only for relevant revisions, preserving previous receipts. Provide export, retention, and deletion rules for receipts. Optional marketing consent must be separate and off by default.

## Decisions required before publication

1. Confirm the legal operator, mailing address, monitored privacy/support email, launch date, governing law, and intended countries/states. The current public contact fallback is `daniel@origenedu.ai`; this is not confirmation that it is the privacy-request channel.
2. Approve and implement minimum-age and guardian rules. The drafts propose 13+ independent use, but no enforceable age gate was established by this review. Under-13 service use may trigger COPPA; an ordinary checkbox does not satisfy verifiable parental consent requirements. Assess treatment of younger student profiles too.
3. Verify production providers, subprocessors, agreements, OpenAI processing/training settings, Render regions/backups/log expiry, Auth0 identity deletion, security access, and incident handling. Replace draft verification notes with accurate public statements; do not assert zero retention or certification without evidence.
4. Establish defensible retention for support records, logs, backups, closure hashes, lock records, legal acceptance receipts, and inactive accounts. Confirm that deletion/export procedures match the Notice, including linked families, institutional records and native mobile.
5. Determine applicable privacy laws based on the actual operator, revenue/data practices, users, and school relationships. Add required regional supplements and request/appeal channels; confirm sale/advertising-sharing statements and GPC obligations. Do not claim CCPA, GDPR, FERPA, or COPPA compliance by publishing a generic agreement.
6. Seek review from a qualified legal friend, legal-aid reviewer, or other appropriate reviewer when available; paid counsel is not a prerequisite to preparing or reviewing these documents. Finalize English and matching Spanish text, then publish stable URLs and implement consent receipts after resolving the factual and operational gaps above. These drafts intentionally omit mandatory arbitration, class waivers, and a blanket monetary liability cap.

## Sources supporting the placement recommendation

- California's Attorney General explains the requirement for conspicuously accessible online privacy policies: [CalOPPA overview](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/facts/online-privacy/privacy-policy).
- The Ninth Circuit discusses conspicuous notice and unambiguous assent in online contracting: [Berman v. Freedom Financial Network](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2022/04/05/20-16900.pdf). The proposed unchecked checkbox is a design recommendation, not a claim that one interface guarantees enforceability everywhere.
- The Attorney General distinguishes a broad privacy policy from notice at collection and describes CCPA applicability and rights: [CCPA guidance](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa).
- The FTC explains under-13 collection, actual knowledge, parental notice, and verifiable parental consent: [COPPA FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions).
