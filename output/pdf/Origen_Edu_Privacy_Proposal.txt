ORIGEN EDU PRIVACY NOTICE PROPOSAL

For students families and community organizations

Prepared October 15 2026 | Proposed notice for review

Origen Edu helps students and families explore college options and organize their next steps. This proposed notice explains the information used to provide that support, what is saved after a conversation, who may receive information, and the choices available to users.

This is a proposal for a public notice, not confirmation that every described control is available in the live service. The notice language requires verification against the deployed product before publication. A separate proposed coordinator-sharing clause describes a planned feature. The final appendix identifies the remaining publication decisions.

The proposed privacy commitment

We use information to provide college guidance, maintain your account and planning history, support sharing that you authorize, and operate and protect Origen Edu. We do not sell personal information or use student profiles or private conversations for advertising targeted across unrelated websites or services.

Browsing a university page does not give that university access to your identity, private profile, or conversations. Family links share designated academic profile information after an invitation is accepted. Private conversations are not automatically shared through a family link. Any future sharing of a summary with a community coordinator requires a separate, specific authorization.

Origen Edu does not save microphone recordings as part of your account history. Live audio is processed by our AI provider to make voice conversations work. We save written summaries of topics discussed and relevant planning context. A summary can contain personal information you provide; it is not necessarily anonymous or limited to a list of topic names.

A name is not the only personal information

A name may help us address you naturally, but account identifiers, email addresses, educational information, questions, and saved summaries can also be personal information. Providing less optional detail reduces the information processed, though it can make guidance less personalized. Voice summaries normally save automatically after a scoped conversation with user speech ends; individual summary approval is not required.

Who this notice covers

The notice covers Origen Edu websites, applications, college-guidance features, and related support. It applies to students, family members, institutional representatives, and other visitors using those services. A community organization does not receive access to student information merely because it recommends or sponsors Origen Edu.

Operator: Blueprint Holdings LLC. Effective date: [publication date]. Privacy contact: daniel@origenedu.ai. Mailing address: [business mailing address]. References to "we," "us," and "our" mean that operator. Feature availability and privacy controls may differ between web and mobile releases.



INFORMATION WE COLLECT AND WHY

Account and sign in information

When you create or use an account, we process information such as your name, email address, account role, and an identifier provided by our sign-in service. We use this information to authenticate you, keep records associated with the correct account, provide support, and protect access. Your sign-in provider processes authentication information under its own terms and settings.

Student and family planning information

Depending on what you enter and the features you use, a student profile may include a name, school or educational stage, GPA, interests, activities, goals, colleges of interest, intended entry term, practical needs, and notes. This information helps tailor guidance and organize plans. Free-text fields can contain additional personal information even when the field does not specifically request it.

Family links create records of the invitation, acceptance, participants, and linked student profiles. We use those records to manage the academic information shared through the link. Do not enter information about another person unless you have appropriate authority and any permission required for that use.

Conversations and saved work

We process your typed questions or spoken statements, the AI responses, selected profile details, and relevant previous summaries to provide guidance. Saved work may include summaries, topic notes, college plans, action items, worksheets, source links, and dates. Where enabled, progress features also record changes to academic information, plan revisions, and self-reported milestones. These records are associated with your account; they are not independently verified school records.

Information you deliberately send to others

If you use an available institution inquiry or event-request form, we process the name, question, topic, contact details if supplied, event information, and permissions you submit. Institutional representatives provide information such as their work email, role, affiliation, and page content. Approved information intended for a public institutional page becomes public.

Technical information and support

We process operational information such as visit and feature-use indicators, language preferences, session identifiers, request timing, error categories, and quality signals to maintain the service and diagnose problems. Our infrastructure providers may receive IP addresses, device and browser information, and other request metadata. These operational records are separate from the aggregate page counts supplied to universities.

If you contact support or make a privacy request, we process the information you send and the records needed to respond. Optional feedback may include your response and associated account or feature context. We do not treat a support request as permission to publish your story or testimonial.

Information you should avoid providing

College guidance does not require Social Security numbers, passwords, bank-account numbers, payment-card information, tax documents, or confidential medical records. Avoid entering or speaking these details. You can discuss financial-aid processes without sharing exact financial identifiers. An educational conversation can still reveal sensitive circumstances, so include only the context you want processed and potentially summarized.



AI GUIDANCE VOICE AND SAVED SUMMARIES

The purpose of the AI

Origen Edu is designed for college-related guidance, including college exploration, academic preparation, admissions, applications, financial-aid information, scholarships, education financing, and planning. Its AI instructions direct conversations toward those topics. This describes the intended scope; it does not guarantee that every generated response stays within it or is accurate.

AI guidance supports your decisions. It does not make admissions or financial-aid decisions for a university, submit an application merely because you discuss one, or establish that you qualify for a program. Verify deadlines, requirements, costs, and eligibility with the relevant institution or official source. A generated statement that you agreed to something is not evidence of your authorization.

What is sent for AI processing

We use OpenAI to generate responses and summaries and support live voice. Relevant questions, conversation text, selected profile information, and past summaries may be sent to OpenAI for those purposes. The service may also compare a summary with the conversation to identify possible quality problems. These operations involve processing personal information even when no audio recording is saved by Origen Edu.

When a feature searches for public college information, it processes the query and relevant search criteria. The application includes measures to limit private details in search inputs, but automated filtering cannot guarantee that all personal information is removed. Avoid adding sensitive details to a search request.

How voice works

Voice is optional and requires microphone permission. While a voice session is active, microphone audio is transmitted to OpenAI for live processing. Speech recognition produces text used to respond and prepare summaries. Stop the session or revoke microphone permission to stop further capture. Stopping does not recall information already transmitted.

Origen Edu does not retain raw microphone recordings in its account database or provide a recording archive for replay. It does retain written summaries and other saved planning records. The application also temporarily processes conversation text and pending summaries so a discussion can continue and an interrupted save can be retried. Temporary session handling is distinct from your saved account history.

What a summary may contain

A saved summary may describe the college topics explored, your goals, relevant academic context, questions that remain open, suggested next steps, and sources discussed. For example, a scholarship discussion could produce a summary of the scholarship types explored and your intended next action. It may also mention a name or personal circumstance supplied during the discussion. Review summaries for accuracy and remove details you do not want retained using available controls or a privacy request.

Provider retention and model training

Not storing recordings in Origen Edu does not mean zero retention by every provider. OpenAI documents that API data is not used to train its models by default unless a customer opts in. It also documents default abuse-monitoring retention of up to 30 days, with exceptions and different rules for some features. Those provider defaults are not proof of the settings on Origen Edu's project. The applicable configuration must be verified before a stronger no-training or zero-retention promise is published.

WHEN CONVERSATION SUMMARIES ARE SAVED

Automatic saving after a voice conversation

When you speak in a voice conversation about a confirmed student or family context, Origen normally generates and saves a written summary automatically when the call ends. You are not asked to approve each summary before it is generated or saved. Ending the call is not a discard action, and reviewing a summary afterward is different from approving it in advance.

The summary may include personal context you supplied, the topics explored, unresolved questions, and proposed next steps. Generated notes may be prepared from a saved summary. Use the available review and deletion controls if you want to remove saved information. The retention choices described in this notice apply to saved summaries and their dependent generated notes.

Profile saving is a separate action

During profile setup, details can appear as a draft while you speak. You can request a profile save with Save profile or by asking Origen to save it. That action concerns the profile; it is not separate approval of each conversation summary. You should not interpret a profile-review instruction as a promise that no other record will be generated or saved automatically.

Deferred saving during onboarding

If the conversation concerns a new student whose profile has not yet been saved, summary generation can still send conversation text to OpenAI before the profile is saved. Saving the written summary to the account may be deferred until that student profile exists. Pending summary work can then be retried automatically while the private session remains valid. This does not add an individual summary-approval step.

The server may retain limited lifecycle, attribution, or quality records associated with a deferred summary even before the summary text is attached to a saved student. Temporary text and pending summaries are distinct from saved account history. Logging out or changing accounts clears private session buffers; closing or reloading the page can lose unsaved conversation text. These actions do not recall information already sent to OpenAI or remove an already saved summary.

Limits and save failures

Automatic saving depends on captured user speech, a confirmed conversation scope, a valid private session, and successful processing and storage. A call without captured user speech or without a confirmed target does not follow the ordinary automatic-summary path. Network failures, interruptions, or a missing profile can delay or prevent a save; the interface can offer a retry. Do not rely on every call producing a complete summary.

For signed-in use, completed summaries belong to account history. In a preview, saved summaries may remain in the browser instead. Neither flow creates an Origen microphone-recording archive. Provider processing and retention remain separate, as explained in the AI and voice section.

Notice before microphone use

The onboarding start control should disclose that microphone audio and the supplied profile details go to OpenAI, and that summaries normally generate automatically when a call ends. This notice should be visible before microphone capture begins, in the selected interface language. The AI's spoken welcome and the browser microphone prompt are not substitutes for that disclosure.



LIMITED SHARING AND ACCESS

No sale and no advertising use of private student records

We do not sell personal information or give advertisers student profiles or private conversation content for advertising across unrelated services. Operating the service still requires limited processing by providers and may involve disclosures you request or that the law requires. The following categories explain those circumstances.

Linked family accounts

A family link is established through an invitation and acceptance. It shares designated academic profile fields, including the student name, stage, school, GPA, interests, activities, goals, selected institutions, and entry term. Linked participants may be able to update shared academic fields. Review the sharing explanation before accepting a link.

A family link does not automatically share private conversation summaries, private needs, or private notes. Linking an account is not proof of legal guardianship or unrestricted authority over another person's records. An adult student's parent or family member does not automatically receive access without authorization.

You can end a family link through available controls. Unlinking stops future synchronization through that link. It does not erase an independent copy of previously shared academic information retained in another account or downloaded by another person.

Community coordinators

Coordinator access to selected summaries is a planned feature. A community organization's referral, sponsorship, or staff role does not currently grant access to a student's private conversation history. The proposed consent-based feature and its proposed public wording appear in the separate proposed coordinator-sharing section. It must be implemented before that wording is presented as an available service.

Institutions you choose to contact

Simply visiting an institution's page does not disclose your private account records to that institution. If you deliberately submit an available question or event request, the designated institution or department receives the submitted information and relevant replies. This is separate from aggregate analytics and does not include your private conversation history.

Where offered, permission to answer a request and permission for future outreach are separate choices. You can withdraw a request or unsubscribe using its available controls. A proposed transfer to another institution or department requires the approval described in the request flow. A recipient may retain information it has already received under its own responsibilities; withdrawal cannot erase independent copies.

Legal obligations and organizational changes

We may disclose information when necessary to comply with a valid legal obligation, investigate misuse, address a security threat, or protect lawful rights. If ownership of the service changes, information may transfer with the service subject to applicable protections and notice obligations. Such a transfer is not blanket permission to use student information for unrelated purposes.

ANALYTICS STORAGE AND SERVICE IMPROVEMENT

What universities see in page analytics

Institution page analytics report aggregate activity, such as page-view and link-click totals. These counters are not a list of student names, email addresses, private profiles, or conversations. They do not identify which individual viewed a page. Visiting a page does not authorize a university to contact you.

The basic page-counter design stores an institution identifier, a calendar month, a metric type, and a count, rather than a visitor identifier. Reports cover completed months and suppress small positive counts below 10. These counts may include repeated visits or clicks and do not establish unique students, applications, admissions, or enrollment. Small-count suppression reduces disclosure risk; it does not prove that every report is anonymous in every context.

If separate inquiry, event, or campaign reporting is available, its aggregate activity measures are distinct from a recipient's authorized access to a request you submitted. For an aggregate-only community or university pilot, individual contact and outreach features should remain outside that pilot's scope.

Operational and quality information

We use operational signals to understand whether sign-in, onboarding, voice connections, language selection, summary saving, and other features work. Quality checks can identify a repeated question, an interrupted response, or a possible discrepancy in a summary. These checks support troubleshooting and review; they are not grades, psychological assessments, or verified measures of student understanding.

The conversation-quality reporting design saves categories, counts, references to turns, and standardized explanations rather than a second transcript or quoted excerpts. A quality check can nevertheless process conversation text before producing those findings. Operational records may remain associated with an account even when a report does not display conversation content.

Optional feedback and progress features, when enabled, can support service evaluation and continuity. Keeping previous summaries for guidance, checking summary quality, conducting research, and training an AI model are different activities. This notice does not treat ordinary use as blanket permission for an unrelated research study or release of student-level datasets. A separate activity requiring consent must have its own explanation and permission.

Browser and device storage

Cookies and browser or device storage support authentication, preferences, preview records, and application operation. Some preview data and preferences may remain after a tab closes. Temporary signed-in conversation buffers and saved cloud records have different lifetimes. Closing a tab is not the same as deleting an account.

On a shared device, sign out when finished and remove downloaded files or preview information you do not want another person to see. Clearing browser storage can remove local information but does not by itself delete cloud records. Similarly, deleting cloud information cannot remove a file someone has already downloaded.

External sites and public content

College links and other external resources lead to services with their own privacy practices. Those sites receive information when you visit or submit information to them. Approved institutional page content is public and may be copied or indexed. Private student planning information is not made public merely because an institution has a page in Origen Edu.



RETENTION DELETION AND YOUR CHOICES

Saved account information

Account profiles, plans, notes, worksheets, and saved summaries generally remain until you delete the relevant records or close the account, subject to the exceptions below. The default summary policy is to keep summaries until deleted. There is no inactivity-based deletion schedule.

Where the Privacy Center is available, you can choose automatic deletion of summaries 30, 90, or 365 days after saving. A shorter selection also applies to older summaries. This setting removes expired summaries and dependent generated notes; it does not automatically delete independent notes, plans, worksheets, or profile information. Scheduled cleanup is not necessarily immediate at the exact expiration time.

Different records have different lifetimes

The basic institution page counters retain the current UTC calendar month and the preceding eleven months. Conversation-quality findings have a 90-day cleanup schedule; findings awaiting a saved summary have a shorter cleanup period. Other technical events, inquiry records, support records, and provider records follow their applicable operational schedules. Optional institution inquiry records expire 90 days after submission and are removed by scheduled cleanup. Withdrawal clears submitted content and related messages earlier, while limited operational records may remain. The other schedules must be confirmed for the published service.

Limited deletion receipts and account-closure safeguards may remain after private content is deleted. They record identifiers or hashed identifiers and deletion information to prevent deleted records from reappearing, including after recovery from a backup. They do not contain the deleted conversation prose, but they are not necessarily anonymous. They currently remain indefinitely unless an operator removes them; no automatic expiry is implemented. The business must approve a bounded schedule tied to the recoverable backup period and necessary safeguards before promising a shorter duration.

What deletion does and does not remove

Deleting a summary can also delete notes generated from that summary. Separately saved notes, plans, exports, other account holders' independent copies, and records held by recipients may require separate action. Closing an Origen account removes supported records from active application storage. It also queues a separate Auth0 identity-deletion request. An authorized operator must complete and verify that request; a pending request is not completed deletion. This does not delete an underlying Google account or every provider security log. The operator must publish an actual processing deadline before promising one.

Backups, infrastructure logs, security records, and records needed to meet a legal obligation may persist for a limited or legally required period. We do not promise immediate erasure from every backup or provider system. Confirmed backup and provider schedules must be included in the operational policy before publication of precise deletion deadlines.

How to use your controls

You can review and edit supported profile information, remove supported saved records, manage family links, stop microphone access, and use available export or account-closure tools. Sensitive operations may require recent sign-in, resolving family links, or review of institutional responsibilities. Contact us if a control is unavailable.

Send privacy requests to daniel@origenedu.ai. Explain the request and the account concerned without sending passwords or sensitive identity documents in an initial message. We may verify identity or authority proportionately. Depending on applicable law, rights may include access, correction, deletion, a copy of information, and other choices. We will explain applicable limitations and any required appeal process and will not penalize you for exercising applicable rights.

GUARDIAN PERMISSION: LOCAL REVIEW

Implementation appendix | Not deployed | Email disabled

How registration works

The neutral English/Spanish screen has no selected answer. It asks the account user for an age band and educational situation before ordinary sign-in, then records those answers against the signed-in identity before profiles, voice or AI. It collects no full birth date. Under-13 users cannot proceed; students aged 13-17 remain pending; adults can use the adult path. Existing accounts without a recorded decision must also complete the gate.

An adult makes a separate decision

A parent or legal guardian reviews a bilingual notice and a request reference, makes an unchecked declaration, and clicks an explicit approval button. Opening an email link does not approve anything. The notice describes voice processing, automatic summaries, separately saved records, withdrawal and deletion. Permission never creates a family link or gives the guardian, a referring organization or a coordinator access to private conversations.

The application keeps the hashed account linkage, age band, request identifier, notice version, decision, revision and server timestamps. It keeps the guardian email for the permission request. Random approval and withdrawal tokens are stored only as hashes in PostgreSQL, not plaintext. The email provider and recipient mailbox necessarily receive the original links. Approval expires in 48 hours; a resend invalidates earlier links. Permission and the separate withdrawal link expire 180 days after the request.

Withdrawal and storage

Withdrawal blocks new permitted use and invalidates older work. It does not itself erase records already saved or recall information sent to a provider. The guardian email is erased on denial, withdrawal or account closure. Hourly cleanup removes pending email and token hashes after 30 days and expired approval details after the 180-day term. Decision events expire after 365 days or earlier account closure. Minimal eligibility status stays until account closure to enforce the restriction.

What the tests establish and what remains

Synthetic tests cover permission decisions, replay, expiry, direct API blocking, delayed writes and provider responses, and PostgreSQL row isolation and concurrent decisions. Web and native source changes are local. Realtime calls have a durable termination queue and periodic provider hangup checks; outages can delay remote termination. Nothing promises immediate recall or zero provider retention.

Email possession and a declaration do not conclusively verify guardianship or establish legal sufficiency. Self-reported age can be false, and direct Auth0 signup may create a preliminary identity before Origen screening. Review identity-provider signup, published wording and any required stronger verification before launch. A separate controlled provider and native-device test is still required.
