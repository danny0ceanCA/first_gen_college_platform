# Institution registration and publication

## Phase 1 institutional access

Institutional access phase 1 is implemented locally. Migration `020_institution_access.sql` adds parent/department relationships, expanded workspace roles, registration retry records, staff invitations, access audit events, and private affiliation requests. Deploy the backend and migration before the updated frontend; readiness now requires migration 020. Production sign-in and a real PostgreSQL acceptance check remain deployment validation steps.

Ordinary sign-in resolves current institution membership before opening family onboarding. Institution-only users open the institutional portal; people with both a personal account and memberships choose a workspace. `#personal` explicitly opens personal onboarding or the personal app. Institution registration does not create a family account. Access-loading failure shows retry and logout controls rather than treating missing data as no membership.

Representatives register a parent institution, department, or program. Departments/programs link to an existing parent using its stable database ID. Parent owners approve external unit requests; their own new units are approved for the relationship automatically. Every unit still requires independent Origen affiliation and publication review. A parent must be verified and published before a child can be published. Parent membership alone does not grant editing or team access to its children. A child cannot be attached to another child or reparented through draft edits. Public pages link approved children to their published parent.

The registration client supplies a UUID `registrationKey` for each new registration and reuses it on retry. The server serializes requests by account, stores a payload fingerprint, and prevents replay from creating a second workspace. A changed payload under the same key returns `registration_changed` so the user can refresh and find the saved registration.

Workspace roles are enforced on the backend:

- Owner: edit and submit content, manage members and invitations, transfer ownership, and approve requests under their parent institution.
- Editor: edit and submit content in assigned workspaces.
- Outreach staff and analyst: view their assigned workspace and permitted aggregate insights. Inquiry handling is a later phase.
- Independent reviewer: verify affiliation and publication through the existing backend reviewer allowlist. Membership in the unit or parent prevents self-review.

Owners create invitation links for a specific sign-in email and editor/outreach/analyst role. Links expire after seven days, are single-use, and can be revoked. Only token hashes are stored. The backend retrieves the bearer token's identity from the configured Auth0 `/userinfo` endpoint, requires matching subject and verified email, and never trusts a browser-supplied profile or work email as evidence of email ownership. The frontend requests `openid profile email`. No Auth0 Organizations subscription or institutional Auth0 role is required. See [Auth0 user information](https://auth0.com/docs/api/authentication/user-profile/get-user-info).

Invitations are shared manually; creating one sends no email or SMS. Transfer requires authentication within five minutes using the existing namespaced auth-time Action. The receiving owner must already be a member; the previous owner becomes an editor. An owner cannot be removed or demoted through ordinary member controls. Transfer invalidates outstanding invitations from the previous owner. Membership is checked on each protected operation, so removal takes effect even with an existing Auth0 session. Access changes are audited without invitation secrets.

People seeking access to an existing institution, or a department under an unregistered parent, can submit a private affiliation request. It does not create a duplicate public institution or grant access. Independent reviewers record verification or clarification, and can link a request to a registered published parent. Existing institution owners arrange access through invitations. A reviewer can mark a linked request resolved only after membership exists. Requests for an unregistered parent remain pending operational follow-up until the parent is established. Representatives can see their requests; reviewers see the review queue. Private requests are included in the requester's account export and are removed with their representative record on eligible account closure.

## Workflow

1. Sign in with the existing Origen Auth0 application. The registration route survives the callback.
2. Register a representative's first name, work email and institution role. These are private operational contact details, not an email-login feature or proof of authorization.
3. Save and preview a draft page: institution name, official HTTPS website, description, programs, admissions guidance, aid resources, events and resource links. A separate optional public contact email is explicitly labeled public.
4. Submit for review. An authorized reviewer independently confirms affiliation through the institution's official contacts/process and records a short non-sensitive note. Nothing verifies itself from a matching email domain.
5. The reviewer reviews the page and approves publication or requests changes. Self-review is prohibited. Public pages render institution-provided text and links, with a notice distinguishing it from independently verified admission guidance.

The work email is not automatically emailed or exposed publicly. There is no SMS/email notification or automated external outreach. Staff need to refresh the review dashboard to see submissions. No staff member is automatically made an administrator.

## Phase 4: consented in-app inquiries

Implemented locally with migration `023_institution_inquiries.sql`. Replies remain in app by the owner’s October 7 decision; no email or SMS is sent. Transactional email, verified recipient setup, provider delivery outcomes and automated retries remain a separate configuration and implementation task. Do not enable outbound delivery by setting a key alone; this version rejects the email channel.

Public approved pages offer bilingual inquiries and event RSVP requests. Forms explicitly name the receiving institution/unit and parent, show the shared fields, require an adult (18+) attestation and permission to respond, and keep optional future opportunities separate. No account identity, family records, voice summaries, research data or email address is attached or collected by the form. RSVP means a request, not a confirmed place. Current approved event IDs and expiry are checked on the server.

Visitors receive a private bearer link at `/#inquiry/<secret>`. The secret stays out of HTTP URLs and server access logs and only its SHA-256 hash is stored in PostgreSQL. Anyone with the link can read or withdraw that request; visitors must save it, since this release has no email recovery or notifications. It allows visitor replies, refresh, future-permission withdrawal, removal of the request and approval/decline of a named transfer. There is no general public listing of requests.

Owner and outreach roles have the inbox for their exact membership institution. Editors, analysts, unrelated accounts, sibling staff and parent owners without unit membership cannot see contacts. Assignments require a current eligible staff membership. Replies and notes have immutable request keys for retry deduplication; stale updates return conflict errors. Internal notes never appear to visitors. Staff sharing a reply confirm the recipient first. In-app status indicates storage/availability, not that the visitor read it.

Transfers are limited to the same parent institution and require eligible staff memberships at both source and destination. The visitor must approve the exact destination and current request revision. Contacts are not visible to the destination until approval. Source-only internal notes are removed from the transferred request; future outreach permission is reset because that grant belonged to the prior recipient. Publication and staff access are rechecked at approval. Consent and operational actions are audited.

Requests expire 90 days after creation, including ongoing cases. They disappear from reads at expiry; hourly cleanup and startup cleanup physically delete them and their messages. Full withdrawal immediately blanks contact and request text, deletes messages, revokes both permissions, and prevents further messages. Minimal institution/action/time/staff audit records remain, with no visitor name or message. Optional future permission is recorded for phase 5; no campaign-sending feature is enabled by phase 4.

Inbox pages use 25-record keyset pagination. Owner-only JSON exports require recent authentication, a recorded purpose, and explicitly include at most the latest 100 unexpired records of that one institution. JSON avoids executable spreadsheet formulas. Exports contain personal operational data; store them only under the institution’s approved handling policy. Export downloads cannot be remotely recalled after withdrawal.

Anonymous requests have a process-local 30 requests/minute TCP-peer budget and a 16 KB JSON body limit; authenticated requests also use the existing account budget. Do not treat this as distributed abuse prevention or an email verification system. Before broad public rollout, validate reverse-proxy addressing and add persistent edge-level controls as needed. Before opening forms to minors, agree and implement the institution’s age-appropriate contact policy; the adult-only attestation is a release boundary, not proof of age.

## Phase 5: campaigns, permission-based invitations and results

Implemented locally with migration `024_institution_campaigns.sql`. Keep backend migration 024 ahead of the frontend deployment. Phase 4’s owner decision continues: invitations are stored in the app at private request links; email and SMS remain disabled. No Auth0 role additions are required. Existing institution membership determines access.

Owners and outreach staff can create campaigns for their own approved page or an upcoming approved event. Links and locally generated QR codes use `/institutions/<slug>?campaign=<UUID>` with optional language/event parameters. Event campaigns cannot run past the approved event’s end. Campaign dates, archive status, page availability, parent publication and event validity are checked on collection and delivery. An expired/archived campaign link still opens the page but new activity has unknown attribution. Invalid or foreign campaign IDs are never adopted. No referrer, visitor ID, email, profile or inferred affiliation is used to assign a campaign.

Browser page views and validated calls to action update aggregate monthly counters. Campaign-specific counters are separate anonymous totals, updated in the same transaction as the workspace total. Public server-rendered share-page views are not counted; campaign links go to the browser app. Inquiry submission records a valid explicit campaign and a metadata-only operational fact in the same transaction as the inquiry. Submission retries do not create additional facts. The inbox shows the campaign title for each attributed request so staff can trace a campaign response without an audience analytics drilldown.

Invitations require both English and Spanish versions, an explicit selection of up to 25 requests, a preview showing each name and its chosen-language message, and confirmation. Only unexpired adult requests with current future-outreach and reply permission in that exact workspace are eligible. No parent/sibling contact inheritance is allowed. The server binds the preview to the authenticated staff member, recipient IDs, revisions, names, languages, campaign and both message texts. Permission and publication are checked again within the send transaction; any changed recipient blocks the whole batch for review. Retries use immutable batch keys and cannot duplicate messages. Limits persist in PostgreSQL: 100 invitations per workspace in a rolling 24 hours and three per private request link. Each opted-in request is a destination, not a unique-person estimate; repeated requests from the same person cannot be deduplicated without additional identity data. In-app storage does not prove reading or notification delivery.

Invitations are tagged separately from request replies. They do not resolve or reopen a request, and do not count toward first-response time. Withdrawal removes its messages; permission withdrawal excludes the request from subsequent batches. Replaying an already completed batch reports its past result but never recreates removed messages. Transfer resets future permission, clears the operational inquiry’s current campaign attribution, and preserves the original campaign/unit only in its reporting fact; parent totals count the original submission once.

Results use only the last completed UTC calendar month. There are no custom date windows, language/audience filters, live deltas or numeric campaign breakdowns. Metrics cover browser page-view events, validated calls to action, new question requests, RSVP requests, that month’s submitted requests unresolved at month end, rounded mean hours to the first request reply before month end, and known/unknown explicit campaign attribution. RSVP is a request, not a confirmed reservation. Counts include repeat activity and possible bots, not unique people, admissions applications or enrollment.

Any current member can see their workspace’s protected aggregate report. Only a root institution owner or analyst can request the parent-plus-departments report; it conveys aggregates, not departmental contact records. Nonzero counts under 10 are withheld. Parent totals are also withheld whenever a contributing unit has a small count. Known/unknown attribution cells are withheld together if either could reveal a small group. Reply averages require at least 10 responses and are withheld for parent totals when a unit’s response group is small. Missing browser counter rows remain unavailable, not fabricated zeroes. Reports show phase-5 collection start, not-collected/partial-period status, and explicitly state that continuous collection uptime is not independently verified.

Metadata-only facts and state history preserve original submission ownership, first reply time and month-end status after contact retention expires. Facts have no name, email, message, language, receipt secret, profile or research fields. They retain a nullable operational inquiry reference only while that inquiry exists. Facts/state history and campaign counters are removed after 12 months; invitation batch content is removed after 90 days. Contact messages still follow phase 4’s 90-day request retention. Campaign configuration records remain for link administration. No research release or model-training permission is granted by this implementation.
